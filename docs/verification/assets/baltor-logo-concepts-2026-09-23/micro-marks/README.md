# Dedicated small Baltor marks

The owner's subsequent references favor a mature dog with a lifted chin and
longer muzzle. The [latest profile variation board](../proud-profile/index.html)
shows three later SVG families at actual size. The frontal studies below
remain as earlier explorations.

The owner found the 16- and 32-pixel reductions of the raster concepts too
detailed and blurry. This study redraws the mark as native SVG shapes on
separate 16 by 16 and 32 by 32 coordinate grids.

Open the [actual-size browser comparison](index.html).

| File | Drawing decisions |
|---|---|
| [Husky at 16 pixels](husky-16.svg) | Five filled paths or shapes: pale tile, large navy head and ears, white face mask and central blaze, aligned eye/nose shapes, one blue ridge. No body, paws, fur cuts, gradients or internal ice facets. |
| [Husky at 32 pixels](husky-32.svg) | The same family with adjusted facial proportions, rounded eye shapes and two plain white paws. It is separately drawn rather than a mechanically enlarged 16-pixel file. |

The larger head occupies most of the available square. The 16-pixel eyes
are two pixels wide and one high, aligned to the grid. Browser
antialiasing still affects curved and diagonal edges; using SVG alone
does not guarantee recognition or eliminate all antialiasing.

The earlier summit-16.svg and summit-32.svg drafts remain as an initial
mask-only exploration. The alternate open-silhouette pair uses three
shapes and is structurally clear, but visually reads too much like a cat
or helmet. It is retained in the comparison as an unsuccessful recognition
test, not the proposed choice.

The comparison shows native display sizes, a browser-tab example, and
32-pixel headers on light and dark surfaces before offering an optional
enlarged geometry view. Local Chromium renders were inspected at a device
scale factor of one. That is a visual design check, not a user-recognition
study. These exploratory marks are not installed as the production logo.
