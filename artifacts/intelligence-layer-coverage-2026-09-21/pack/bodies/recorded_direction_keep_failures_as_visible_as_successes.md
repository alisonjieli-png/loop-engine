## The guidance as recorded

Preserve failures and excluded attempts with the same prominence as successes.
Save every report under a new name. Keep failed attempts beside their
successors.

## What it asks a step to do

Do not overwrite a report when you run again. Write the new one under a new
name and leave the old one in place. When an attempt was excluded from a
result, record that it was excluded and why, next to the result, not in a
footnote or a separate file nobody opens.

The prominence part is the part with teeth. A failure recorded in a place
where the reader has to go looking has been recorded and hidden at the same
time.

## Why it changes what a later reader can conclude

A single passing report proves that something passed once. A passing report
next to the three failures that came before it proves considerably more: that
the path was hard, where it broke, and whether the pass came from a repair or
from avoiding the difficulty.

## Response expected from a step

Every attempt saved under its own name, the exclusions listed beside the
result with their reasons, and the count of attempts stated rather than
implied.

## Limits

This is guidance, not permission and not proof. It does not permit editing a
historical report, a private session log, benchmark evidence, a managed
record, or a failure baseline. Preserving failures means adding records, never
rewriting the ones already there.
