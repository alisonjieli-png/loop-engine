## The guidance as recorded

Marketing language does not have to be measurable, and treating it as though
it must produces flat, lifeless copy that says nothing. There are two
categories and only one of them needs evidence.

Write freely: evaluative and aspirational words, energy, and a strong point of
view. Also write freely about what the product is designed to do and about how
it works, because the mechanism is real and can be read in the source.

These need evidence every time: a number nobody measured, a comparison to a
named product, the words guaranteed, always, never, every or any applied to an
outcome, a customer or testimonial or user count that does not exist, and a
capability the product does not have.

## The test

Ask whether a reasonable reader would take the sentence as a fact they could
verify. If not, it is enthusiasm and it is fine. If yes, it needs data.

## Why the correction was needed

Copy written under the assumption that every word must be measurable ends up
describing nothing, because the interesting sentences all get removed. The
correction restores the distinction rather than lowering the standard: the
standard for checkable facts did not move.

## Response expected from a step

A step drafting public copy should be able to point at each sentence that a
reader could check and name its evidence. It does not have to justify a
sentence that carries no checkable fact.

## Limits

This is guidance, not permission and not proof. It does not permit a claim
about a benchmark, a provider, or a measured benefit that saved evidence does
not support.
