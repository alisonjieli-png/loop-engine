## The guidance as recorded

State observed, inferred, assumed, missing, and disputed facts separately.

## What it asks a step to do

Sort every sentence in a result into one of five boxes before writing it.

1. Observed. Something ran and this was its output.
2. Inferred. This follows from something observed, and the reasoning is
   stated.
3. Assumed. This was taken as true without checking, and the reason is stated.
4. Missing. This was needed and could not be obtained.
5. Disputed. Two sources disagree, and both are named.

The five look similar in a finished paragraph, which is exactly the problem.
Prose blends them for free. Separating them costs a little effort once and
saves a reader from acting on an assumption they took for a measurement.

## Response expected from a step

A result that labels its facts, or groups them under headings that carry the
five labels. A missing fact stated as missing rather than filled with a
plausible value. A dispute preserved with both sides rather than resolved
quietly in favour of the more convenient one.

## Why "missing" has its own box

A missing fact that is silently dropped becomes an absence in the report, and
an absence reads as a fact that did not matter. Naming it keeps the gap
visible to the next person, who may be able to close it.

## Limits

This is guidance, not permission and not proof. Labelling a claim as observed
does not make the observation correct, and labelling one as assumed does not
make the assumption safe.
