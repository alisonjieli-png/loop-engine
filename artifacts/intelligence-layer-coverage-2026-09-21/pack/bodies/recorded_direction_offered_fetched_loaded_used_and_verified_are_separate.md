## The guidance as recorded

Offered, fetched, loaded, used and verified are separate facts. A count of
passing checks is not a working customer journey.

## What it asks a step to do

Report the fact you actually observed, and only that one.

1. Offered. The item appeared in a list of things available.
2. Fetched. The bytes were transferred.
3. Loaded. The material was read into the place that will use it.
4. Used. The work actually consulted it and the result depended on it.
5. Verified. Somebody other than the producer checked the result.

Each step is easy to confuse with the next. A search result proves the item
was offered. A download proves it was fetched. Neither proves a model ever
read a word of it, and none of the first four proves it helped.

## Response expected from a step

A named fact from the five, the evidence for that fact, and silence about the
ones not observed. When a claim needs a later fact than the one observed, the
right answer is that the later fact is missing, not that it probably holds.

## Why the second sentence is in the guidance

A green suite is evidence about the suite. A customer journey is a different
population: a real person, a real account, a real client, real material, and a
real result. Counting checks and reporting a journey is the substitution this
direction exists to refuse.

## Limits

This is guidance, not permission and not proof. It sets what may be claimed.
It does not itself establish any of the five facts for any item.
