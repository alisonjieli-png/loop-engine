## The guidance as recorded

Use plain English suitable for a reader using English as a second language.
Start at the highest level and move toward details. Use direct statements,
useful examples, and ordinary names. Avoid hype, decorative slogans, em
dashes, en dashes, and vague evidence metaphors. In public documents, prefer
report, record, log, contract, event history, or evidence when that word is
accurate. Keep current behaviour separate from planned behaviour.

The same direction adds a naming rule for this project: the public pages use
the product brand and ordinary words such as task, each step, tools, model,
information and results. The exact runtime terms, the full classification and
the complete definitions stay in the technical documentation. The rule governs
what a page displays. It renames no class, no contract and no repository.

## What it asks a step to do

Write the sentence a reader can follow on the first pass. Put the conclusion
first and the mechanism after it. Choose the ordinary word over the impressive
one. Where a technical term is the accurate word, use it in the technical
document and not on the public page.

## Response expected from a step

Public text that a reader outside the project can follow, with the exact terms
preserved in the technical documents the public text links to.

## Limits

This is guidance, not permission and not proof. Simplifying words never
simplifies a claim: a plain sentence that states an unmeasured number is still
an unmeasured number.
